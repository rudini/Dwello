# Native French read (Romandie): statement PDF, reminder letters and e-mails (#84, option B)

Reader: native French speaker from Romandie, Swiss rental practice. Read: `pdf-sample.txt` and `letters-sample.txt` (sections fr), the `fr:` block and `FLOORS` in `backend/src/services/pdf.service.ts`, `UNITS` in `statement-pdf.service.ts`, the `fr:` block in `reminder-letter.ts`, and `name_fr` in `migrations/0002_stammdaten.sql`. Binding terms (`docs/design/i18n-begriffe.md`) are kept as they are. None is plainly wrong, so no binding term is flagged.

Overall: a Romand tenant understands both documents. The QR-bill labels match the SIX French wording. The legal references (art. 257b al. 2 CO, art. 8 OBLF) are right. What remains: a few Germanisms ("n’est pas encore payé", "Nous vous virons"), missing non-breaking spaces throughout the PDF, one wrong ordinal ("1e sous-sol"), and some telegraphic master-data names.

## C. Could not confirm / observations

- The French samples print the German master-data names: «Hauswart», «Pro Fläche (m²)». `localName` (statement-pdf.service.ts:52) falls back to `name_de` when `name_fr` is empty. The seeded rows all have a `name_fr` («Concierge», «Par surface (m²)»), so the sample probably comes from a fixture with no French names. I could not confirm whether a landlord's own category or key gets a `name_fr`. If it does not, a French tenant sees German on the statement.
- The rendering of U+00A0 in the PDF fonts (FR-8) is inferred from `printable()` in pdf.service.ts, which accepts the range \xa0–\xff, and was not checked visually.
