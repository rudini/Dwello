# Native English read (British): statement PDF, reminder letters, e-mails (#84, Option B)

Reader: native British English speaker, expat tenant in Switzerland. Read: the `en` rendered samples (PDF due/refund, reminder levels 1–3), every `en` string in `pdf.service.ts` (`translations.en`, `FLOORS.en`), `statement-pdf.service.ts` (`UNITS`), `reminder-letter.ts` (`WORDING.en`), and `name_en` in `0002_stammdaten.sql`.

Overall: the texts are clear and mostly natural. The reminder letters read like a real British/Swiss property-management letter. There are no Majors in the samples. The weak spots are in strings the samples do not trigger (the per-invoice calculation lines, heating and fuel notes and floor names), plus German-style guillemets and one term mismatch ("receipts" against the binding "supporting documents").

SIX QR-bill labels (`Payment part`, `Receipt`, `Account / Payable to`, `Payable by`, `Acceptance point`, `Additional information`, `Separate before paying in`) are the official SIX English wording. They are correct and must stay as they are.

## Observations (no row: binding terms or not confirmed)

- **Sample shows German names in the English PDF**: the `en` samples print "Hauswart" and "Pro Fläche (m²)" (as do fr/it). `statement-pdf.service.ts` `localName()` picks `nameEN`, so the sample generator probably passes the German names directly to `pdf.service`. I could not confirm this on a real generated statement. A real one should print "Caretaker" and "Per Area".
- **Binding term "utility costs"** (Nebenkosten): to a British reader, "utilities" means gas, electricity and water, while this statement also covers the caretaker, garden and lift. Swiss English sources usually say "ancillary costs" or "additional costs". It is not plainly wrong, so it is not flagged. It is noted for whoever owns `i18n-begriffe.md`.
- **Binding term "time share"** (Zeitanteil): in British English "timeshare" means holiday-property shares, which gives a faint unintended association. Understandable in context, so it is not flagged.
- **"VMWG"** in `noteHeating`/`noteStock` is a German abbreviation that an English reader cannot place. "CO" for OR is the official Swiss English abbreviation. I could not confirm the official Fedlex English abbreviation for SR 221.213.11 (the web search returned nothing relevant), so I propose no change.
- **Dates** dd.mm.yyyy (17.03.2026) are unambiguous in Swiss practice and fine for an expat. A British reader would also accept them.
- **"centimes"** is fine in Swiss English (official SIX/SNB English also uses "centimes"; "Rappen" would also do).
- **Letters**: apart from EN-23 to EN-25, the reminder wording is idiomatic and in the right register ("According to our records …", "You may have overlooked the payment", "please quote it with your payment", "please disregard this reminder", "Kind regards").
